## MVP planning fields

### Assumption one

Young Austin concertgoers will make a meaningful choice among rising-artist event formats when the
options are presented equally.

### Assumption two

At least 5 of 10 target users will send an affirmative first-access response after seeing a
proposed $20 general-admission price.

### Assumption three

Completed price-intent responses and purchase-context answers will give D42PE a better early
booking signal than views, likes, or verbal compliments alone.

### Riskiest assumption

At least 5 of 10 Austin high-school or college-age concertgoers will actually send the prepared SMS
selecting `Join First Access at $20` after choosing a rising-artist event format.

### One-sentence MVP description

My MVP is a four-step mobile concept test where young Austin concertgoers choose a rising-artist
event format, respond to a proposed $20 price, explain what drives their decision, and send their
completed answer to D42PE by text.

### Components and why

- Mobile concept page: tests whether a user understands and completes the idea without coaching.
- Three equally presented event formats: measures which type of rising-artist night has the strongest preference.
- Proposed $20 price with yes, maybe, and no choices: forces a more realistic decision than asking whether the idea sounds cool.
- Audience and recent-purchase questions: distinguishes target users from people with no relevant buying behavior.
- Purchase-factor and critique questions: explains why each user made the choice.
- Prepared SMS response: creates a deliberate final action and a verifiable response without adding a backend or collecting payment.
- Source tag: distinguishes Instagram, Snapchat, and directly recruited testers in each completed text.

### Included now

- One mobile-friendly concept page
- Three rising-artist event-format choices
- A visible proposed $20 general-admission price
- Yes, maybe, and no price-intent choices
- Target-user and recent-event context
- Purchase-factor, artist-suggestion, and critique questions
- Instagram, Snapchat, and direct source attribution
- A prepared SMS and copy fallback
- Clear disclosure that no artist, event, date, or venue is confirmed and no payment is collected
- A 10-person directed test

### Saved for later

- Named artists, clips, or artist applications
- Artist and fan accounts
- Automatic rankings or recommendations
- Stored signups and a backend database
- Ticket payments or checkout
- Confirmed dates, venues, or lineups
- Artist contracts and payout systems
- A promoter dashboard
- A full artist marketplace

### Success and failure thresholds

- Supported: at least 5 of 10 invited target users send the completed SMS with `Join First Access at $20`.
- Inconclusive: 3 or 4 of 10 send that affirmative response.
- Not supported: 0, 1, or 2 of 10 send that affirmative response.
- Separately record how many of the 10 send any completed response. A missing text is not a completion.

## Ten-user protocol

Use exactly 10 Austin high-school or college-age people who attend or seriously consider paid
events. Do not count family, participating artists, or D42PE collaborators.

1. Send every tester the same direct link without explaining the desired answer.
2. Record the invitation before they open it so the denominator remains 10.
3. Ask them to complete the page and press send in Messages. Opening the prepared text is not a completed response.
4. Do not persuade them to choose yes. A no or maybe is useful evidence.
5. After they finish, ask what was unclear or untrustworthy and what one change would improve it.
6. Count only texts actually received by D42PE.
